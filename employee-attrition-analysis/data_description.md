# Data Description

All data is **synthetic**. It is generated by `src/data_generator.py` with numpy and a fixed seed (42). The files are saved to `data/`. Value ranges below are enforced by the generator (clipping or by construction).

## Table `employees.csv`

One row per employee (3000 rows).

| Column | Type | Meaning | Values / range |
|--------|------|---------|----------------|
| `employee_id` | integer | Unique employee identifier (primary key) | 1 to 3000 |
| `department` | categorical (string) | Department of the employee | Sales, Engineering, Support, HR, Finance, Marketing |
| `gender` | categorical (string) | Gender of the employee | Female, Male, Non-binary |
| `age` | integer | Age in years | 22 to 60 |
| `job_level` | integer | Seniority level, 1 = junior, 5 = most senior | 1 to 5 |
| `years_at_company` | float | Tenure at the company in years (1 decimal) | 0.0 to 35.0 |
| `monthly_income` | integer | Monthly gross salary (synthetic currency units) | 2500 to 20000 |
| `overtime` | categorical (string) | Whether the employee regularly works overtime | Yes, No |
| `distance_from_home_km` | integer | Commute distance in kilometres | 1 to 50 |
| `num_projects` | integer | Number of projects the employee currently works on | 1 to 7 |
| `years_since_last_promotion` | float | Years since last promotion (1 decimal); never exceeds `years_at_company` | 0.0 to 35.0 |
| `attrition` | integer (binary) | Target: 1 = employee left the company, 0 = stayed | 0, 1 |

No missing values.

## Table `satisfaction_surveys.csv`

One row per employee who answered the satisfaction survey. Not every employee responded (the response probability is 0.92), so the table has fewer than 3000 rows. Employees without a survey row must be handled as missing when joining.

| Column | Type | Meaning | Values / range |
|--------|------|---------|----------------|
| `employee_id` | integer | Employee identifier (foreign key to `employees.employee_id`, unique in this table) | 1 to 3000 |
| `job_satisfaction` | integer (ordinal) | Satisfaction with the job, 1 = low, 4 = high | 1 to 4 |
| `work_life_balance` | integer (ordinal) | Self-reported work-life balance, 1 = poor, 4 = excellent | 1 to 4 |
| `manager_support` | integer (ordinal) | Perceived support from the direct manager, 1 = low, 4 = high | 1 to 4 |

No missing values inside the table; missingness appears only as absent employees.

## Relationships

- `satisfaction_surveys.employee_id` references `employees.employee_id` (many-to-one in principle, one-to-one in practice).
- Overtime lowers job satisfaction and work-life balance in the generator; attrition depends on overtime, satisfaction, income, promotion gap, commute distance, age, tenure and the Sales department, plus random noise.
